## What it is and my role
ReLease is a two-sided marketplace where verified renters review their landlords. It is live at [joinrelease.com](https://joinrelease.com).

I am the sole founder and operator. I built and launched it solo, end to end. Nobody else worked on it.

## The problem
A review marketplace launches with zero supply. The incumbent, och.berkeley.edu, is operated by CoStar and cannot carry reviews.

## What I did
1. Ran a competitive teardown of CoStar's och.berkeley.edu and found it cannot carry reviews.
2. Built and launched the product: an address directory with reviews from renters who verify their email.
3. Ran one outreach batch to seed reviews.
4. Audited production read-only instead of trusting my own status document. The audit overturned an earlier all-clear: ten 'reviews' were QA data and the takedown contact was unset, so I paused seeding until both were fixed.
5. Diagnosed a {{release_spam_signups}}-signup surge in three days as form spam (dot-injected Gmail addresses, out-of-state ISPs) instead of reporting it as growth, and shipped double opt-in. (My own account as of {{release_spam_signups:date}}, not re-measured.)
6. Priced, then killed, my own recommendation (LRS certification, parked three weeks after I proposed it), and rejected my own idea of memes as a format on evidence.

## What went wrong
ReLease is live, but I under-invested in distribution and over-invested in the build. What I would do differently is set the supply target before the build target, not after.
